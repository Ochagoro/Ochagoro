Don't come for the waterfalls

Yosemite Falls is essentially dry by August — the snowmelt that feeds it is finished. Only Bridalveil, Vernal and Nevada run year round, and they run thin. Treat August as the month for granite, light and rivers rather than for falling water, and the whole trip gets better.

The valley bakes, the high country doesn't

The valley floor (4,000 ft) hits 90–97°F in the afternoon. Glacier Point (7,214 ft) and the Tioga Road country around 8,000 ft sit near 75°F by day and drop into the low 50s at night. Any plan that puts you in the valley at midday will fail. Spend the heat in water or shade; walk when it's cool.

No entry reservation in 2026

The peak-hours reservation system is gone for 2026 — you can drive in whenever you like. The crowds didn't go anywhere, though, so the real limit is parking. Valley lots fill between 9:00 and 10:00 on a summer weekend.

Assume there's no signal

Outside a patch of Yosemite Village, phones don't work. Download maps, this page and your music on the lodge wi-fi tonight. Agree out loud on a "if we lose each other, meet here at this time" before you go in.

03 — Elevation

Up 5,000 ft and back
down, in one day.

The story of tomorrow is altitude, not distance. You leave a lodge at 3,050 ft, spend the day near the 4,000 ft valley floor, look up at a waterfall from 4,400 ft, then climb to 8,127 ft in a single afternoon. The higher you go the colder it gets and the harder the sun bites. Think of the jacket and the water as equipment for the height.

07:35Bridalveil Fall · 30 min

One of the few still falling in August

Half a mile round trip on a gentle paved grade. In summer it earns its name — thin enough that the wind carries it sideways. Arrive first and the viewing deck is yours. The rock underfoot is wet year round and slick, so don't step past the platform.

09:00Mist Trail · 2–3 hours

The one real climb: Vernal Fall

Closed on ThursdaysThe Mist Trail's stairway above the John Muir Trail junction is closed Mon–Thu, 7:00–15:30 for work running through October. Climbing those steps at 9:00 is not an option tomorrow. A and B below account for that.

Shuttle to Happy Isles. The path to the footbridge is open regardless of day or hour.

A｜to the footbridge 1.6 mi ↑400 ft about 1.5 h
B｜top of the fall via the John Muir Trail 5 mi ↑1,300 ft about 4 h

A is the one to take. The first mile is paved, and the view up at Vernal Fall from the footbridge at 0.8 miles is the most reliable falling water you'll see this month. Turn around there and you've still had your walk in the valley.

B detours around the closure and climbs the John Muir Trail switchbacks instead. More than double the distance and the climb, in genuine August sun. What you get for it is Nevada Fall from Clark Point, which looks like Niagara from up there. Bring two litres a person, and accept that Glacier Point is off the table.

To save your legsSkip the Mist Trail entirely for Cook's Meadow Loop — one flat mile. Standing in the middle of the meadow looking up at Half Dome and the Yosemite Falls cliff is the most luxurious walk in the valley, and it leaves something in the tank for Glacier Point.

Eat the sandwiches you already bought, on the sand beside the Merced. Queuing for a restaurant is a poor use of the hour, and the riverbank is better company in this weather anyway. Nothing gets left behind — bears and ravens both work this beach.

13:00Two hours of heat · get in the water

Don't walk through the hottest part of the day. Swim.

The valley pushes toward 95°F this afternoon. Force a hike through it and you'll be empty when the good light arrives. Go to Sentinel Beach or Swinging Bridge and get in. The August Merced is slow and warm — the easiest swimming of the year here.

And put binoculars on El Capitan. Somewhere on those 3,000 feet there are always a few parties. Spot a portaledge with someone asleep on it and you've won the afternoon.

17:15Sentinel Dome or Taft Point · 1.5 h each

One more walk before the light goes

Sentinel Dome (2.2 mi round trip, ↑400 ft, 8,127 ft) is the high point of the day. Standing on the bare granite dome there is nothing between you and 360 degrees, from Half Dome to the far Sierra. Taft Point (2.2 mi round trip, near flat) trades that for deep fissures cut into the rock and an unfenced drop. If heights don't bother you, Taft hits harder.

SafetyTaft Point has almost no railing. Stay two metres back from the edge, never step backward while framing a shot, and hold onto children. People are hurt here every year.

19:45Sunset 20:05 · the peak of the day

Half Dome turns pink

For ten minutes before the sun goes, the granite throws back reddened light and the whole valley flushes rose. The real show is the fifteen minutes after sunset, not the moment itself. Stay until the colour drains out of the sky. The temperature falls off a cliff of its own, so fleece and a windshell.

20:35Start down · about 2 h 10

Take the night Sierra slowly

Glacier Point Rd → Wawona Rd → Big Oak Flat Rd → Hwy 120. This drive is the price of this plan. Deer, bears, switchbacks, a long descent. Hold the speed limit, pull into a turnout if you're fading, and swap drivers if there's anyone to swap with.

If you stop onceA turnout on Glacier Point Rd, or Washburn Point. With no moon, the Milky Way in August is plainly visible to the naked eye. Kill the engine, give your eyes five minutes, then look up.

22:45Back at the lodge

Seventeen hours.

Lucky Buck Cafe shuts at 22:00, so this is too late for it — dinner is whatever you picked up at midday. Leave no food in the car — bears work parking lots too.

To get an hour more sleepGive up Glacier Point and take sunset at Tunnel View instead. That's about 1 h 20 back to the lodge, in by 21:30, which gets you dinner at Lucky Buck Cafe (open to 22:00). Less of a view, half the night driving, and a hot meal at the end of it.

Fill the tank in Oakdale

In-park gas is Crane Flat and Wawona only and it's dear, and Groveland isn't cheap either. Fill up in Oakdale on the way through. Tomorrow is about 130 miles of driving.

Buy tomorrow's food and water

Breakfast, lunch, trail food, water, ice. Mar-Val Food Stores, 19000 Main St in Groveland, 7:00–22:30. Buying inside costs more and costs you time in a queue.

Download offline maps

On the lodge wi-fi, save the Yosemite area in Google Maps and download Yosemite in the NPS app. Bookmark this page too.

Dinner at Lucky Buck Cafe

Attached to the lodge, 7647 State Hwy 120, open until 22:00 May–Sept, (209) 962-5181. Tomorrow you're back at 22:45, so this is the last real dinner.

Empty the car of food

Bears come through the Buck Meadows lot too. Food, snacks, lip balm, sunscreen — anything with a smell goes inside.

Finish packing, set the 4:45 alarm

The morning should be nothing but wash your face and get in the car. Fill in the checklist above before you sleep.

09 — Park rules

Four that cost you
if you get them wrong.

Bears

Nothing edible visible in the car

Yosemite's bears open cars. Trailheads have food lockers, so use them. It isn't only food — it's anything that smells: lip balm, sunscreen, empty wrappers, rubbish. There are fines for getting this wrong.

No signal

Plan as though phones don't exist

Outside part of the valley, they don't work. Save maps offline and agree on a meeting place and time out loud before you go in. In an emergency, the nearest visitor center or a ranger. Airplane mode makes the battery last.

Parking

Park once, don't move until dark

Valley lots fill during summer days. Assume that leaving means not getting back in. Move on the free shuttle or a rented bike. In-park speed limits (25–45 mph) are enforced properly.

Heat

Don't walk the valley at midday

August in the valley is around 95°F. The biggest risk of the day isn't a fall or a bear, it's heat exhaustion. Between 11:00 and 15:00 be in the water, in the shade, or indoors. Drink before you're thirsty. Headache or nausea means shade, immediately.
